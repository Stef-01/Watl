Work

Described by structure,
not by logo

Most of it sits under agreement. What follows is the shape of the work, and one piece you can inspect line by line.

01 — Piece

Bloom of
Remembrance

A gilded figure and a seed pod, standing in a field of wattle. Rendered live, sixty times a second, from about two thousand lines of code.

It began as a single flat poster. Turning it into something that holds up while a visitor swings the lens twelve degrees meant rebuilding every part of it as description rather than as pixels — and that turned out to be the interesting bit.

The figure's proportions are measured, not invented: head twenty-one per cent of its height, neck thirteen, torso forty-five, legs twenty-two. A long body over short legs under a long neck. Get those four numbers wrong and the shading cannot save it.

Every motif is drawn to an offscreen canvas at load, from a fixed seed — the vine climbing the chest, the fern held inside the pod, the lace along the serpents, the craters in the moon. Nothing ships as an image, so nothing is the wrong resolution and everything recolours when the theme flips.

The material is not physically based. A renderer that spends its budget conserving energy is the wrong tool for an illustration, so the light is written by hand: one soft key from above-front, a warm bounce off the field below, a rim term to hold the contour, low-frequency mottle so the gradients read as beaten leaf, and aerial perspective tied to the live camera distance.

You are looking at it on the index page. The source is on GitHub.

Geometry

Lathes, extrusions, ribbons

The head is a lathe with a hand-authored profile, pressed flat in z. The torso is an extruded tapered slab with a real bevel. The pod is a mandorla with the field cut out of it, so the frame has thickness on both edges. The serpents are ribbons built along a curve, width running along tangent × viewward so the band lies open to the camera.

Field

Three depths of bloom

Instanced billboards behind, beside and — the detail that makes the depth read — in front of the bodies. Each carries a tint from highlight through gold to shadow. A field of highlights alone reads as fog; the reference has genuine shadows in it.

Behaviour

It answers you

The lens follows the cursor on a spring, looser in yaw than in pitch, so it reads as a head turning rather than a gimbal. The body warms as you close on it. Touch it and the field throws petals while the vine relights from the root.

Fallbacks

Three tiers and a still

Quality is decided once per device — instance counts, pixel ratio, whether the glow pass exists. No WebGL, and a flat SVG of the same composition holds the frame. Reduced motion renders exactly one frame and never touches the animation loop again: a considered still, not an absence.

02 — Shapes

Four ways
this goes

Scope and fee fixed before we start. No hourly billing — it rewards the wrong behaviour on both sides.

Two weeks

One image

A single piece that has to carry an argument: a hero, a cover, a conference plate. Delivered as source and as stills at every size you need.

Six weeks

An identity

Mark, type, colour, motion, and the written standard. Tokens first, so the system arrives in a form your engineers can actually consume.

Twelve weeks

A site or a product surface

Designed in the browser against real copy, real data and real accessibility requirements. Handover is working front-end code, not a file to be reinterpreted.

Retained

A standing hand

A monthly block for organisations that keep needing the next thing looked at. Unused time does not roll over; we would rather you cancelled than banked it.

03 — Limits

What we
will not do

Produce a logo without the system it has to live in. Design a screen against placeholder copy. Ship an animation that cannot be turned off. Take a brief where the decision is already made and the design work is cover for it.

We also will not hand over a static export of something that should have been generative. If the thing wants to be a program, it gets built as one.
